# BRIGHT SIDE (@BRIGHTSIDEOFFICIAL) — Channel Teardown

## 1. What the channel actually is

| | |
|---|---|
| Owner | TheSoul Publishing / TheSoul Group (Cyprus) |
| Launched | 2017 |
| Scale | ~44.7M subscribers, ~11.6B lifetime views |
| Format | 100% faceless: stock/animation + single voice-over narrator |
| Core length | 8–10 minutes of read-aloud script (~1,200–1,600 words) |
| Cadence | Daily / multiple per day (the parent company has produced ~1,500 videos a month across its network) |
| Genres | Survival & body facts, space/science, historical mysteries, life hacks, "what if" scenarios, riddles & tests |
| Biggest hit | "13 Tips on How to Survive Wild Animal Attacks" — 90M+ views |
| Newest big genre | Riddles/detective puzzles (since 2018), top entries at 37–39M views |

The channel is a **content factory**, not a personality channel. Nothing depends on a face, a
location or a schedule. That is exactly why it is copyable.

## 2. The scenario templates (they reuse ~6 of them forever)

1. **Numbered survival / danger list** — "N Tips on How to Survive X". Their highest ceiling.
2. **What-if hypothetical** — "What If [big thing] Happened Tomorrow?" One scenario, escalating consequences.
3. **Body & mind facts** — "Why Your Body Does X" — everyone owns a body, so the audience is infinite.
4. **Space & scale** — planets, black holes, the ocean floor. Cheap visuals, huge curiosity payoff.
5. **Historical mystery** — an unsolved thing, several theories, no firm conclusion (keeps comments alive).
6. **Riddle / test** — "Only 4% Can Solve This". Interaction bait; viewers pause, guess, comment.

## 3. The hook system

Title formulas they cycle through:

- `[Number] + [Surprising thing] + You Never Knew`
- `Why [ordinary thing] Is Actually [unexpected thing]`
- `What Happens If [scenario]?`
- `[Number] Signs That [relatable situation]`

Thumbnail rules: **2–4 words max**, power words (SECRET, TRUTH, NEVER, ALWAYS), readable at
phone size, and it *complements* the title instead of repeating it. Big arrow, one circled
element, high-saturation colour.

## 4. The script skeleton (this is the actual money)

```
0:00–0:20  HOOK      A surprising question or a "you're doing it wrong" claim.
0:20–0:35  PROMISE   Tease the single most counterintuitive item, but don't reveal it.
0:35–7:45  BODY      7–12 items, 30–45 seconds each. Every item:
                     claim → why it's true → what to do / vivid image
                     Between items: "But wait, there's more…" / "And that's not the weirdest part."
7:45–8:15  RECAP+CTA Compress the list, then ask a question to force comments.
8:15–8:30  ENDCARD   Two more videos, subscribe line.
```

Language level: **8th grade**. Short sentences. Second person ("you", "your"). Tone: friendly,
curious, slightly amazed — never lecturing.

## 5. Why it retains

- **Open loop in the first 10 seconds** (the tease that only pays off near the end).
- **Reset every 35 seconds.** A new number = a new micro-hook, so there is never a flat stretch.
- **Best item is never #1.** The strongest fact sits around 70–80% of the runtime.
- **Zero dead air.** Something on screen changes every 3–5 seconds.
- **Second-person stakes.** It's not "people should", it's "*you* would have about 60 seconds".

## 6. What to copy — and what not to

Copy: the structure, the pacing, the numbered format, the thumbnail discipline, the open loop.

Don't copy: their accuracy problem. Bright Side is widely criticised for clickbait and shaky
science. That's the one thing that caps a channel's long-term trust — and it's free to fix.
Curiosity-driven titles that the video actually *delivers on* keep the same CTR and don't burn
the audience.

## 7. Practical production stack for a clone

- Script → VO (real narrator or high-end TTS)
- Visuals → stock library + simple motion graphics, cut on every beat
- One template project file, so each episode is assembly, not design
- Batch: write 5 scripts, record 5 VOs, edit 5 videos — never one at a time
